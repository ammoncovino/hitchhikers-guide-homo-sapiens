CHAPTER FOUR

# Your Personal Position

Why I Can See This From Where I Stand, and Why I Almost Didn’t Say It

I want to tell you about the day I deleted my Instagram posts. Not because it was dramatic. I just sat with my phone, looked at what I had posted, and quietly made it disappear. That quiet moment is the most honest thing I can tell you about where I stand, and why this book exists.

### What I Posted and Why I Stopped

I had been watching the war. Watching the numbers. Watching the language used to describe what was happening, the compression, the abstraction, the careful avoidance of the word children when children were dying. And I was posting about it. Then something shifted. I am not a poor man. I live in a social class, a network, a position that most people who might read posts like mine do not live in. And I realized: I was living in the Capitol.

> In the Hunger Games, the Capitol is where the wealthy live, the people who watch the suffering at a comfortable distance, who feel things about it, who maybe even oppose it, but whose opposition costs them nothing and changes nothing.

Posting on Instagram from the Capitol is not resistance. It is the performance of resistance. So I deleted the posts. Not out of fear. But because I made a strategic decision: be against it where it counts, not where it’s visible. Quietly, through money, through access, through the people I can reach, help the people who are actually doing something about it.

### The Capitol Is a Real Place

I mean it structurally. There is a class of people in this country who exist in a different relationship to the government than most people do. That position comes with a specific temptation: to use it only for yourself, and to perform concern for the people outside it while doing nothing that threatens your position. And it comes with a specific responsibility: to use the access, the assets, and the position to actually change things.

### The War and the Primitive Problem

On the morning of 28 February 2026, a missile struck the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in Minab, in southern Iran. Somewhere between a hundred and seventy and two hundred and sixty children were inside. Amnesty International concluded that U.S. forces were responsible, found a Tomahawk likely visible in footage of the wider attack on the adjoining compound, and stated expressly that it could not independently verify whether the school itself was struck by that same munition. That distinction is kept here because this book is about to ask other people to be careful with evidence. [6]

Then the part you need to understand before anything else in this book will land properly.

After the first strike, a teacher and the principal moved a group of surviving students into the prayer hall, which is what you do. The principal then telephoned the parents and told them to come and collect their children, which is also what you do.

The second missile hit the prayer hall.

One of the parents later described getting that call, being told his daughter had survived, and setting out to fetch her. The school was struck again before he arrived. Two Red Crescent medics and that parent gave this account to Middle East Eye. Minab’s mayor and the Iranian Ministry of Education state the school was struck three times in total. Satellite analysis by BBC Verify indicates the area was hit with multiple missiles. [7]

This has a name in military vocabulary. It is called a double tap, and when there is a third strike, a triple tap.

The tactic works like this. You strike a target. You wait. You do not wait long, only long enough for the thing you know will happen to happen, which is that human beings will run toward the wreckage. Medics. Neighbors. Fathers who received a phone call. Then you strike the same place again, because the second strike is not aimed at the target. It is aimed at the rescue.

A third strike catches whoever comes for the second group.

> The tactic is designed around a specific piece of knowledge: that human beings run toward the injured. It does not merely accept that as a cost. It requires it. The mercy is the targeting mechanism.

Sit with that, because it is the most complete inversion in this book. Everything that makes a species survivable, the reflex to help, the willingness to enter danger for a stranger, the mother who goes back in, is converted into a weapons guidance system. The compassion is what makes the second missile worth firing.

The counts are source-dependent and should not be merged into one number. Amnesty used an official total of a hundred and fifty-six dead including a hundred and twenty children. Airwars independently identified a hundred and fifty-seven dead, including a hundred and twenty-three children and thirty-four adults, and placed the possible total between a hundred and fifty-seven and a hundred and sixty-eight. Of the adults identified, twenty-six were school staff and five were parents. Many died in the second strike, in a prayer hall, waiting for the parents who had been telephoned. [7]

The Pentagon’s own preliminary assessment concluded that American forces were responsible, and that the strike came from a targeting error. The Defense Intelligence Agency had the building classified as a legitimate military target, a classification carried over from years earlier when the site had been part of an adjacent naval compound. The classification was stale. Nobody checked it. The missiles were precision-guided and they hit exactly what they were aimed at.

That is not a statistic. That is a crime, and it is a specific kind of crime, the kind that happens because a line in a database was never updated and no human being in the chain was required to look at what the line actually pointed to.

Here is what nobody outside the system knows, and it is the part that matters more than the numbers.

Somewhere there is a record of which ship fired those three missiles. There is a name on the order. There is a name on the intelligence product that carried the stale classification, and a name on the review that passed it, and a name on the approval that released the weapon. None of that is unknowable. All of it is written down. The United States Navy does not lose track of which vessel launched a Tomahawk.

It is simply not ours. The chain of command that killed a hundred and twenty-three children is fully documented and entirely invisible to the people it was done in front of, and an investigation was announced that was expected to take months.

> A system that can tell you the exact serial number of the missile and not the name of the person who approved firing it has not lost the information. It has decided which half of it you get.

> We are on a rock flying through the universe at hundreds of thousands of miles per hour. There is almost certainly nothing else like us out here. And we are spending our time killing each other over whose grandfather’s story about God is correct.

The people prosecuting these wars believe they were chosen, that an ancient covenant gives them a category of existence that supersedes the category of the people they are killing. This is the Arc from Chapter Two running at civilizational scale, dressed in scripture, backed by missiles, sustained by a self-fulfilling prophecy we will examine in Chapter Seven.

### Why It Is Your Problem

Someone reading this will think, reasonably enough, that a school in Hormozgan province is not their business. They did not fire anything. They do not know anyone who did. There is a war on the other side of the world and they have a shift tomorrow.

But look at what we actually are. Not a collection of separate people who happen to share a planet. A single living system, running on the same machinery, dependent at every point on other parts of itself. You did not grow the food you ate today. You cannot make the antibiotic that will save you, or the concrete under your house, or the language you are reading this in. Every one of those arrived through other people, most of them strangers, most of them dead. That is not sentiment. That is the literal supply chain of your continued existence.

An ecosystem does not have bystanders. When something is damaged in one part of it, the damage does not stay there, and no part of the system gets to declare itself unrelated. A body does not send white cells only to the wounds it approves of.

> So when one human harms another, the obligation to see it answered does not belong only to the injured party. It belongs to everyone downstream, which is everyone. Justice is not a service the victim purchases. It is maintenance the whole organism performs on itself.

That is the entire reason accountability has to be personal and public rather than institutional and sealed. An institution cannot be held responsible, because an institution cannot be shamed, cannot be imprisoned, and cannot lie awake. Only a person can. When a system arranges things so that a decision was made by a process rather than by anybody, it has not distributed the responsibility. It has dissolved it, and the people it was dissolved on behalf of go on eating dinner.

A hundred and twenty-three children were killed by a decision a specific human being signed. Somewhere that name exists. Wanting to know it is not bloodlust. It is the minimum functioning immune response of a species that claims to be one.

### Where AI Comes In

Artificial intelligence has the potential to make the mechanisms of power legible to the people living under them, in real time, at scale. Every decision a government makes leaves a data trail. What I want to see is a generation of people who know how to use these tools to build accountability into government at a structural level, to make every decision traceable, every authorization something a human being is personally and publicly accountable for.

Without this chapter, the book is just theory. I wrote this book because visibility, actual visibility into how these systems work, might be the most useful thing I can do with where I happen to be standing. We are on a rock flying through the universe. There is probably nothing else out there like us. Make the best of it.
