### INTRODUCTION

Two things are easy to confuse, and almost everything that follows depends on keeping them apart: the world as it actually is, which does not care what anyone believes about it, and the account of that world you were handed, which is the only version you have ever had direct contact with. You cannot reach the first one unmediated, because everything arriving at you has already been described by somebody, already selected, already named, and that is why the account does not feel like an account at all but simply like the world. The first chapter gives both of those things their proper names and takes them apart properly, so for the moment the distinction itself is enough.

The book runs in five parts, and they build rather than repeat. Part One supplies the tools: how an account of the world gets installed before you are able to assess it, three moves that turn up in every system ever built to direct people, and the way language does most of the installing. Part Two watches those tools operate on an individual, through physical position, architecture, inherited scripture and prophecy. Part Three is the same machinery at full size, in empire, institutional capture, money and debt. Part Four is the mirror, where every mechanism in this book turns out to have been sold back to us already as entertainment that we paid to watch. And Part Five is the exit, which is the only part that asks anything of you.

Three things will help while you read, and the first is a willingness to be uncomfortable, because some of this will collide with something you hold and that discomfort is not evidence the book is wrong. It is what examination feels like when the thing being examined has never been examined before, and it passes.

The second is the ability to hold more than one thing at once, because the systems described here contain real meaning and real manipulation simultaneously, and the temptation is always to resolve that into all sacred or all corrupt simply because a single verdict is easier to carry than two facts that will not reconcile.

The third is to test all of it against your own life, since the only frameworks worth keeping are the ones that make your own experience more legible than it was before. If a chapter does not do that, set it down, and that includes this one.

> Nothing here is asking to be believed. It is asking to be checked, which is a different request, and the only one worth making.
